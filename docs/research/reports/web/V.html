V · Heterogeneous small-model consensus

Web-sourced prior art, tools, papers and articles relevant to this direction. Links are real web-search results; relevance notes are the researcher's synthesis.

V1 — A 2-3 family small-model panel beats any single family at equal per-model budget

Mixture-of-Agents Enhances Large Language Model Capabilities (arXiv 2406.04692)
Foundational MoA paper: layered aggregation across multiple different LLMs beats the best single model (65.8% vs 57.5% on AlpacaEval 2.0) — the core "diversity covers blind spots" argument behind a multi-family panel.
Rethinking Mixture-of-Agents: Is Mixing Different LLMs Beneficial? (arXiv 2502.00674)
Important kill-test counterpoint: Self-MoA (aggregating only the single best model) often beats cross-family MoA (+3.8% avg), because mixing in weaker families dilutes quality. Directly stress-tests whether family diversity actually helps at equal budget — must be controlled for in V1's design.
SYMPHONY: Synergistic Multi-agent Planning with Heterogeneous LM Assembly (arXiv 2601.22623)
Explicitly assembles a heterogeneous panel of smaller models for planning, arguing complementary strengths; an architecture template for a small-model panel under a per-model budget.

V2 — Inter-model agreement is a calibrated confidence signal

Learning to Trust the Crowd: A Multi-Model Consensus Reasoning Engine (arXiv 2601.07245)
Builds confidence from cross-model consensus and shows non-parametric calibration (isotonic regression) across heterogeneous LLMs beats simple majority voting — direct support for "agreement level → calibrated TP rate" and feeding the K2 confidence knob.
Self-ensemble: Mitigating Confidence Mis-calibration for LLMs (arXiv 2506.01951)
Shows ensembling reduces individual-model miscalibration and yields better-calibrated confidence than self-reports — methodology for validating monotone TP-rate-vs-agreement.
Evaluating and Calibrating LLM Confidence on Questions with Multiple Correct Answers (arXiv 2602.07842)
Practical recipe for measuring whether confidence maps to accuracy (TP rate) and deriving a calibration curve — the exact validation protocol V2 needs to confirm agreement is calibrated.

V3 — Disagreement-routing: only contested findings get expensive verification

Dynamic Model Routing and Cascading for Efficient LLM Inference: A Survey (arXiv 2603.04445)
Surveys cascade/routing where queries escalate to expensive models only when cheap models disagree; documents FrugalGPT (up to 98% cost cut) and Agreement-Based Cascading (ABC, training-free, escalate on ensemble disagreement) — exactly V3's "auto-accept unanimous, verify contested" pattern.
C3PO: Optimized LLM Cascades with Formal Cost Guarantees (arXiv 2511.07396)
Label-free cascade with provable cost/accuracy guarantees — shows you can hold final precision while spending the expensive tier only on hard cases, the cost-vs-precision tradeoff V3 measures.
LLM Cascades with Mixture-of-Thoughts for Cost-Efficient Reasoning (arXiv 2310.03094)
Uses inter-sample agreement as the cascade decision rule (accept if consistent, escalate if not) — concrete instantiation of disagreement-gated escalation that lowers total cost at held accuracy.
