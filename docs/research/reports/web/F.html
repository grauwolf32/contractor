F · Measurement rigor (so the rest is provable)

Web-sourced prior art, tools, papers and articles relevant to this direction. Links are real web-search results; relevance notes are the researcher's synthesis.

F1 — Raising to pass@≥5 with reported variance changes the ranking of prompt/tool variants

Don't Pass@k: A Bayesian Framework for Large Language Model Evaluation (arXiv)
Argues pass@k is sample-inefficient and high-variance for ranking; proposes Dirichlet-prior posterior means + credible intervals as a more stable comparator — directly supports reporting variance bands rather than point pass@1–3.
Pass@k Metrics for LLM Evaluation (survey/topic page)
Summarizes the HumanEval unbiased pass@k estimator (n≥k samples) and the variance-reduction rationale for larger n — the mechanical justification for going to pass@5–10.
PythonSaga: Redefining the Benchmark to Evaluate Code-Generating LLMs (arXiv)
Empirical case that low sample counts and weak benchmarks compress real model differences into noise — analogous to the project's "1-in-7 vulns, best-of-3" concern.
A dive into how pass@k is calculated (Medium)
Practical walkthrough of the unbiased estimator and why naive 1−(1−p)^k overestimates; useful when re-implementing pass@5–10 with σ.

F2 — Adding cost (tokens) + latency as first-class scored metrics surfaces hidden regressions

Evaluation and Benchmarking of LLM Agents: A Survey (arXiv)
Survey enumerates token-cost and latency (TTFT, end-to-end) as standard first-class agent metrics alongside success rate — the template for promoting totals.tokens/latency into the scored envelope.
Benchmarking Multi-Agent LLM Architectures: Cost-Accuracy Tradeoffs (arXiv)
Explicit cost-accuracy Pareto study of orchestration patterns; shows quality-neutral changes that double cost — exactly the "neutral change is actually a cost regression" signal F2 wants to expose.
SWEnergy: Energy Efficiency in Agentic Issue Resolution with SLMs (arXiv)
Studies small-model agent efficiency (tokens/energy per resolved task); directly relevant to the 27b–80b mission and the quality-per-token re-ranking idea.
How Token Efficiency Impacts LLM Cost, Latency, and Scale (CodeAnt)
Defines token efficiency = useful output / total tokens consumed; a ready operational definition for the quality-per-token headline metric.

F3 — Wiring idle corpora as held-out fixtures detects overfitting in codereview v3

Benchmark Data Contamination of Large Language Models: A Survey (arXiv)
Comprehensive survey of how benchmark leakage inflates scores and detection methods — frames the seen-vs-held-out recall gap as the standard overfit/contamination signal.
CapBencher / "How Can I Publish My LLM Benchmark Without Giving the Answers Away?" (arXiv)
Proposes a built-in alarm for test-set overfitting via held-out private sets and repeated-query detection — methodology for treating eyeballvul/Real-Vuln-Benchmark as a held-out tripwire.
On Leakage of Code Generation Evaluation Datasets (arXiv)
Documents leakage specifically in code-eval datasets; supports the worry that a tuned codereview v3 may be fitting seen fixtures rather than generalizing.
Search-Time Data Contamination (arXiv)
Newer angle on contamination when models/agents can retrieve answers at inference; relevant because the agent has web/file access that could leak fixture solutions.

F4 — Fixing the envelope writer makes analytics-ui reflect the real XBOW 14/15

From Large to Mammoth: Comparative Evaluation of LLMs for Vulnerability Detection (NDSS)
Peer-reviewed example of rigorous per-fixture aggregation with TP/FP/FN bookkeeping and 95% CIs; the kind of ground-truth-per-dir accounting a rebuilt envelope should reproduce.
Vulnerability Benchmarks & Evaluation (topic survey)
States the rigor baseline: 95% CIs, P/R/F1 when TP/FP/FN available, severity-weighting — a checklist for what the corrected top-level envelope must surface beyond a raw 14/15 count.
