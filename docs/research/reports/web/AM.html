AM · Stop conditions & iteration-budget sizing

How to set min_iterations per task (the author observed larger projects need a larger floor) and when to stop iterating. Links are real web-search results; relevance notes are the researcher's synthesis.

AM1 — min_iter should scale with project size / attack surface (validate the observation)

STADS: Software Testing as Species Discovery (TOSEM 2018)
Models testing as discovering "species" (behaviors/branches): a larger program has more feasible species, so reaching the same coverage needs more sampling — the formal basis for "larger projects ⇒ larger min_iter." Also estimates the extra effort to cover more.
Large Language Monkeys: Scaling Inference Compute with Repeated Sampling
Coverage scales log-linearly with attempts; the budget needed to hit a target coverage grows with the size of the solution space — quantitative backing for sizing the iteration floor to the task, not a flat constant.

AM2 — auto-derive the budget from cheap structural signals (pre-run)

Scaling LLM Test-Time Compute Optimally (Snell et al., 2024)
Compute-optimal scaling allocates test-time compute adaptively per prompt by difficulty — >4× more efficient than flat best-of-N. The analogue: derive each task's iteration budget from its difficulty/size signals (attack surface, #endpoints, complexity hotspots) instead of one global number.
STADS (estimate effort to cover more)
STADS estimates the additional testing time required to cover more branches — i.e. a principled, structure-grounded budget estimate the planner could compute up front from project size.

AM3 — dynamic saturation / dry-streak stop beats a fixed count

Green Fuzzing: A Saturation-Based Stopping Criterion (ISSTA 2023)
Stops when coverage of potentially-vulnerable functions saturates — ends campaigns 6–12h early while missing <0.5 of 12.5 bugs. The model for a "stop when K iterations yield no new (vuln-relevant) findings" dry-streak rule, smarter than raw coverage.
The Saturation Effect in Fuzzing (John Regehr)
Practitioner account of saturation and why naive coverage/crash plateaus mislead — argues for splitting effort into k subsets and stopping when all agree, a robustness trick for a convergence-based stop.

AM4 — compute-optimal allocation: more iterations to hard/large tasks, fewer to easy

Snell et al. — allocate by difficulty under a global budget
For easy/medium tasks reduce compute; concentrate it on hard tasks needing exploration. Under a fixed total iteration budget, difficulty-weighted allocation should beat a uniform min_iter.
Adaptive Inference-Time Compute: LLMs Can Predict if They Can Do Better
The model itself predicts whether more sampling will help — a cheap per-task difficulty signal to size the budget and trigger early stop on easy tasks.
BEACON: Bayesian Optimal Stopping for Efficient LLM Sampling
Frames "how many samples" as Bayesian optimal stopping — sample until the expected value of another sample drops below its cost; the principled form of the iteration cap (ties Direction U and AL5).

AM5 — residual-risk estimation: a principled stop with a confidence statement

STADS — Good-Turing residual risk
Uses the Good-Turing estimator to bound the residual risk that a vulnerability exists when none has been found — turning "are we done iterating?" into a quantified statement ("≤X% chance of a missed class"), not a gut call.
Estimating Residual Risk in Greybox Fuzzing (Böhme et al., FSE 2021)
Refines residual-risk estimation under adaptive (biased) sampling — directly relevant since the planner's memory-guided iteration is adaptive; gives a defensible stop threshold and a coverage/assurance metric for the eval.
